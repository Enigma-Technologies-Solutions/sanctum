        How this works and what it does not protect
        
        
          
      • The scheme. Shamir's secret sharing works on each byte of the secret in a field of 256 values. Any k shares rebuild the secret. Fewer than k reveal nothing about it, however much computing power they have, as long as the random numbers were good.
        • 
          
      • Randomness. Random numbers come from this window's secure random source. If it is missing, the tool stops instead of using something weaker.
        • 
          
      • Two formats. A Sanctum share adds the number of shares needed, a set id, a checksum on the share and a check value hidden inside the secret. The tool can then tell you when a share is mistyped, missing or from another set. These checks are not secret, so they do not prove the secret is the original. A plain share has none of this and matches ssss-made-easy and the shamir-secret-sharing package.
        • 
          
      • Not the ssss command line. These shares do not work with the ssss-split and ssss-combine programs.
        • 
          
      • What it covers. It protects against lost and mistyped shares. It does not protect against a dishonest share holder, malware, screen capture or another program reading the clipboard. Someone holding enough other shares can invent a share that rebuilds a secret of their choice, so the checks catch accidents, not cheating.
        • 
          
      • Length. Every share is about as long as the secret. The length option pads the hidden data so the exact size is not visible.
        • 
          
      • Cleanup. Nothing is stored. Clear empties the fields and the bytes this page holds, where it can. The idle timer only hides the secret again after 60 seconds without typing. Browsers may keep copies of text in memory, so treat this as best effort.
